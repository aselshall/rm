## Project 1: Hydrogeological Characterization of an Agricultural Field Site

### Supervisors

Ahmed S. Elshall, Ph.D. and Mewcha Gebremedhin, Ph.D.
U.A. Whitaker College of Engineering and The Water School
Florida Gulf Coast University

### Background and Motivation

Southwest Florida’s agricultural soils are predominantly sandy, with limited water- and nutrient-holding capacity. Rainfall and irrigation can rapidly transport water and dissolved nutrients below the crop root zone and into shallow groundwater. Understanding this process requires a clear characterization of the soil profile, shallow groundwater system, hydraulic properties, and local meteorological conditions.

This project will establish the baseline hydrogeological conditions of the agricultural research site at the University of Florida/IFAS Southwest Florida Research and Education Center in Immokalee. The site will support a larger research project investigating whether humic acid can improve water and nutrient retention while allowing reductions in nitrogen and phosphorus fertilizer application. The larger project requires soil-moisture monitoring and shallow groundwater wells to evaluate nutrient movement below the root zone. 

Students will use instruments available through the [Surface Water-Groundwater Field Lab](https://aselshall.github.io/gwh/lab) to characterize the site. These include an ATMOS 41W weather station, TEROS soil-moisture sensors, groundwater-level loggers, a Solinst water-level meter, the SATURO automated in-situ permeameter, surveying equipment, and the ABEM Terrameter LS 2 Electrical Resistivity Tomography system. 

The project will integrate several field methods to develop a three-dimensional conceptual model of water flow through the soil and shallow groundwater system.

### Project Objective

The objective is to characterize the soil, vadose zone, and shallow groundwater system at the Immokalee agricultural field site and determine how water moves through and beneath the site.

### Key Research Questions

1. What soil layers and subsurface features can be identified using Electrical Resistivity Tomography?

2. How do soil texture and saturated hydraulic conductivity vary across the site?

3. How do rainfall and irrigation affect soil moisture at different depths?

4. How does the shallow groundwater table respond to rainfall, irrigation, and seasonal meteorological conditions?

5. What are the groundwater-flow direction and hydraulic gradient across the field site?

6. Are observed variations in electrical resistivity consistent with measured soil particle-size distributions, soil moisture, and saturated hydraulic conductivity?

7. How should the hydrogeological characterization inform the placement and interpretation of future nutrient-leaching monitoring locations?

### Field Data Collection

#### 1. Weather and Soil-Moisture Monitoring

Students will install and operate:

* One METER ATMOS 41W weather station.
* TEROS soil-moisture sensors or soil-moisture profile probes at selected locations and depths.
* A METER ZL6 data logger and cloud-based data-management system.

The monitoring system will provide continuous records of meteorological conditions and changes in soil-water content. These observations will be used to evaluate soil-moisture responses to rainfall and irrigation.

#### 2. Groundwater Monitoring Wells

Three shallow groundwater monitoring wells will be installed at locations that provide adequate spatial coverage of the field site.

Students will:

* Document well construction, depth, screened interval, and soil conditions encountered during installation.
* Survey the horizontal position and top-of-casing elevation of each well.
* Measure groundwater levels manually using a Solinst water-level meter.
* Install water-level loggers where appropriate for continuous monitoring.
* Convert depth-to-water measurements into hydraulic head 
* calculate the hydraulic gradient and groundwater-flow direction using the three-point method.
* Examine whether groundwater-flow direction changes following rainfall, irrigation, or seasonal water-table fluctuations.

The three wells will also support later groundwater-quality monitoring associated with the humic acid field experiment. The larger project proposes collecting shallow groundwater samples within and adjacent to agricultural plots to evaluate nutrient leaching. 

#### 3. Electrical Resistivity Tomography

Students will conduct one or more ERT surveys using the ABEM Terrameter LS 2.

The surveys will be designed to:

* Identify changes in subsurface electrical resistivity.
* Delineate possible soil layers and textural changes.
* Estimate the depth and configuration of the shallow water table.
* Identify zones that may promote preferential water movement.
* Support the selection or evaluation of monitoring-well and sensor locations.

ERT interpretations will be compared with soil samples, groundwater observations, and direct hydraulic measurements. The instrument is designed for imaging subsurface geological structures and groundwater under field conditions. 

#### 4. Saturated Hydraulic Conductivity

Students will use the METER SATURO automated in-situ permeameter to measure near-surface saturated hydraulic conductivity at several locations.

Measurements will be used to:

* Quantify spatial variability in infiltration and saturated hydraulic conductivity.
* Compare hydraulic properties among different portions of the site.
* Examine relationships between hydraulic conductivity, soil texture, and ERT-derived resistivity.
* Identify locations where rapid vertical movement may increase the risk of nutrient leaching.

Because SATURO primarily characterizes near-surface soil, its measurements will not automatically be assumed to represent aquifer-scale horizontal hydraulic conductivity.

#### 5. Soil Particle-Size Analysis

Soil samples will be collected from selected depths and locations and analyzed using the METER Group PARIO automated soil particle-size analyzer.

The analysis will determine:

* Sand, silt, and clay fractions.
* USDA soil-texture classifications.
* Vertical and spatial variability in soil texture.
* Relationships between soil texture, saturated hydraulic conductivity, soil-moisture behavior, and electrical resistivity.

### Data Analysis Approach

The analysis will include:

* Quality control and visualization of weather, soil-moisture, and groundwater-level time series.
* Calculation of groundwater-head elevations from surveyed well elevations.
* Three-point calculation of groundwater-flow direction and hydraulic gradient.
* Analysis of water-table responses to rainfall and irrigation.
* Processing and inversion of ERT measurements to produce two-dimensional resistivity sections.
* Statistical and graphical comparison of resistivity, particle-size distribution, soil moisture, and saturated hydraulic conductivity.
* Spatial mapping of well locations, sensor locations, hydraulic properties, and groundwater elevations.
* Development of a site conceptual model showing soil layers, the water table, groundwater-flow direction, and likely water-movement pathways.

### Why It Is Important

The humic acid project is intended to quantify whether improved soil water and nutrient retention can reduce nitrogen and phosphorus leaching from agricultural land. Interpretation of those results requires knowledge of the site’s natural hydrogeological variability. Differences in soil texture, hydraulic conductivity, groundwater depth, and groundwater-flow direction could otherwise be confused with treatment effects.

This characterization will therefore:

* Establish baseline conditions before the full agricultural experiment.
* Improve the placement of wells and soil-moisture sensors.
* Support interpretation of nutrient concentrations measured in groundwater.
* Identify preferential flow zones or heterogeneous soil layers.
* Provide parameters for future water-flow and nutrient-transport modeling.
* Create a long-term monitoring framework for the Immokalee research site.

The work also reflects the Surface Water-Groundwater Field Lab’s broader emphasis on water-budget measurements, hydraulic characterization, hydrogeophysical imaging, and the fate and transport of agricultural nutrients in South Florida. 

### Expected Outputs

* A hydrogeological characterization report or manuscript.
* A surveyed site map showing wells, sensors, ERT transects, and sampling locations.
* Soil-texture and saturated hydraulic-conductivity datasets.
* Weather, soil-moisture, and groundwater-level time series.
* Groundwater-elevation maps showing hydraulic gradient and flow direction.
* Interpreted ERT cross-sections.
* A three-dimensional conceptual model of the site.
* Recommendations for monitoring nutrient leaching during the humic acid field experiment.
* A final research presentation summarizing the methods, results, limitations, and implications.
